# Tegmentovermian (brainstem–vermis) angle — fetal posterior fossa

> Angle on a midsagittal view of the fetal brain between the dorsal surface of the brainstem and
> the ventral surface of the vermis. It quantifies upward (dorsal) rotation of the vermis and is
> used, together with morphology, to separate Blake pouch cyst, vermian hypoplasia and
> Dandy-Walker malformation. Normal and abnormal values differ markedly between the US and
> MRI series; the numbers are not interchangeable. [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 3]
> [volpe-bv-angle-2012] [nagaraj-dandy-walker-mri-2021]

## 1. Scope

- **Applies to:** midsagittal fetal brain images — US ("brainstem–vermis angle", BV angle) and
  MRI ("tegmentovermian angle", TVA). Both names describe the same two lines.
  [volpe-bv-angle-2012] [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 3, Fig S3]
- US series: abnormal fetuses at 19–28 weeks, controls at 20–24 weeks [volpe-bv-angle-2012];
  3D US volumes at 15–18 weeks [contro-open-fourth-ventricle-2014].
- MRI series: 121 normal fetuses at 18–37 weeks on sagittal T2 [chapman-posterior-fossa-mri-2018];
  33 abnormal fetuses at 1.5 T, mean GA 24.66 (SD 4.8) weeks, all with postnatal neurological
  follow-up [nagaraj-dandy-walker-mri-2021].
- **Does not apply to:** postnatal imaging (none of these sources gives postnatal values); fetuses
  before about 18–20 weeks without caution (§5); diagnosis from the angle alone (§5).

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Tegmentovermian angle (TVA) | Angle between a line along the dorsal surface of the brainstem, up to the nucleus gracilis, and a line along the ventral surface of the vermis | [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 3, Fig S3] |
| Brainstem–vermis (BV) angle | The same angle measured on median sonographic views | [volpe-bv-angle-2012] |
| Dandy-Walker malformation (DWM) phenotype | Vermian hypoplasia (mainly inferior); dorsal vermian rotation with increased TVA; inferolateral displacement of the tenia–tela choroidea complex; obtuse fastigial angle; unpaired caudal lobe | [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 4] |
| Blake pouch cyst (BPC) | Delayed or incomplete opening of the foramina of Magendie and Luschka: enlarged fourth ventricle, tenia–tela choroidea complex displaced superiorly and staying caudal to the vermis; vermis normal | [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 4, Table 1] |
| Mega cisterna magna | Cisterna magna > 10 mm anteroposterior at MRI, without hydrocephalus; diagnosis of exclusion | [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 5] |
| Cisterna magna (normal) | Measured sagittally from the declive to the calvarium; normal range 2–10 mm | [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 3] |
| Vermian hypoplasia (Nagaraj study definition) | Vermis small for GA, TVA > 18°, posterior fossa not enlarged | [nagaraj-dandy-walker-mri-2021] |
| Blake pouch remnant (Nagaraj study definition) | Vermis normal for GA, TVA > 18° | [nagaraj-dandy-walker-mri-2021] |

## 3. Criteria / categories

### 3.1 Normal values

| Modality, population | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| US, 80 normal fetuses, 20–24 weeks | 9.1 ± 3.5° (range 4–17°) | [volpe-bv-angle-2012] |
| 3D US, 93 normal fetuses, 15–18 weeks | 84 (90.3%): closed fourth ventricle and BV < 20°; 9 (9.7%): open fourth ventricle and BV 20–37° | [contro-open-fourth-ventricle-2014] |
| MRI, 121 normal fetuses, 18–37 weeks | 2.5 ± 2.3°; independent of gestational age | [chapman-posterior-fossa-mri-2018] |
| MRI, upper limits as cited by Hiremath (attributed to Chapman 2018) | 12° before 24 weeks; 8° after 24 weeks | [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 3] |
| General statement | Acute, near 0°, in most fetuses after 18–20 weeks | [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 3] |

### 3.3 Cut-offs proposed by the authors

| Setting | Cut-off and meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| US, from mid-gestation | BV > 45° strongly suggests DWM; < 30° favours BPC | [volpe-bv-angle-2012] |
| US, 15–18 weeks | BV ≥ 45° carries a very high risk of severe posterior fossa malformation | [contro-open-fourth-ventricle-2014] |
| MRI | > 18° = elevated (study inclusion threshold, taken from the US work of Volpe) | [nagaraj-dandy-walker-mri-2021] |
| MRI | ≥ 80° helps separate classic DWM from VH and Blake pouch remnant, regardless of GA; severe forms > 80°, milder forms > 18° | [nagaraj-dandy-walker-mri-2021] |

## 4. Management

- None of these sources gives management by angle value.
- US is the first-line examination; fetal MRI is second-line, to confirm and characterize a
  suspected malformation; 3 T is preferred over 1.5 T. [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 3]
- Posterior fossa cystic malformations may be hard to diagnose before 22–23 weeks, and follow-up
  imaging may be needed. [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 2]
- When imaging is insufficient for DWM, describe the morphology in detail and avoid "variant",
  "continuum" and "spectrum". [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 4]

## 5. Pitfalls

- **US and MRI numbers differ.** In the same diagnoses, the MRI series reports far higher angles
  than the US series. For example, the MRI mean for vermian hypoplasia (52°) is above the US DWM
  cut-off of 45°, and the MRI normal mean (2.5°) is well below the US normal mean (9.1°). The
  sources do not explain the difference; the cohorts, gestational ages and referral severity
  differ. Do not apply the US cut-offs to MRI, or the reverse. (Comparison made for this entry;
  no source makes it.) [volpe-bv-angle-2012] [chapman-posterior-fossa-mri-2018]
  [nagaraj-dandy-walker-mri-2021]
- **Early gestation.** At 15–18 weeks, about 10% of normal fetuses have an open fourth
  ventricle with BV 20–37°, and their scans look strikingly similar to those of BPC.
  [contro-open-fourth-ventricle-2014] A transiently enlarged posterior fossa fluid space at
  14–16 weeks resolves at about 22–23 weeks. [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 2]
- **Overlap.** US ranges overlap between BPC (19–26°) and vermian hypoplasia (24–40°)
  [volpe-bv-angle-2012]; the MRI standard deviations are 18–20° [nagaraj-dandy-walker-mri-2021].
- **Small, selected samples.** Volpe: 31 abnormal fetuses. Nagaraj: 33 fetuses, single centre,
  retrospective, TVA measured in 6 DWM; the > 18° inclusion threshold may have excluded smaller
  Blake pouches. [volpe-bv-angle-2012] [nagaraj-dandy-walker-mri-2021]
- **The angle is not the diagnosis.** Hiremath separates DWM from BPC mainly by the position of
  the tenia–tela choroidea complex: a normal position effectively excludes DWM. Torcular position
  is no longer a diagnostic criterion for DWM. [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 4]
- **Mass effect.** BPC and mega cisterna magna may moderately compress the vermis, with
  preserved cerebellar height and vermian ratio. [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 5]
- Nagaraj does not describe the landmarks; it refers to the method of Chapman et al.
  [nagaraj-dandy-walker-mri-2021]

## 6. What the source does not say

- **A combined category scheme** circulates in tertiary summaries (for example a Radiopaedia page,
  screenshot supplied by the radiologist on 2026-11-13): DWM > 45°, vermian hypoplasia and BPC
  18–45°, mega cisterna magna < 18°, arachnoid cyst 0°. None of the sources read gives this
  scheme:
  - Volpe gives separate BPC and vermian hypoplasia ranges and the 30° and 45° cut-offs, for US.
  - The 18° threshold appears in Nagaraj, taken from Volpe, as a lower bound for an elevated TVA.
  - No source gives a numeric value for mega cisterna magna or arachnoid cyst.
  [volpe-bv-angle-2012] [nagaraj-dandy-walker-mri-2021] [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, Table 1]
- **Hiremath gives no cut-off by diagnosis**; Table 1 is qualitative.
  [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, Table 1]
- **The Volpe cut-offs are US cut-offs.** The abstract does not extend them to MRI.
  [volpe-bv-angle-2012]
- **The 12° and 8° MRI limits are not in the Chapman abstract**, which reports a single mean
  (2.5 ± 2.3°) independent of gestational age. They may be in the full text (not seen).
  [chapman-posterior-fossa-mri-2018] [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 3]

## 7. Disagreements and newer versions

- **GA dependence of the normal MRI value:** Hiremath gives two upper limits split at 24 weeks;
  the Chapman abstract says the TVA is independent of gestational age. Unresolved until the
  Chapman full text is read. [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 3]
  [chapman-posterior-fossa-mri-2018]
- **What counts as abnormal on MRI:** Nagaraj uses > 18° (from US data), while the MRI normal
  mean is 2.5 ± 2.3° and the limits cited by Hiremath are 8–12°. Nagaraj itself notes the
  contrast between its threshold and the Chapman normal data. [nagaraj-dandy-walker-mri-2021]
  [chapman-posterior-fossa-mri-2018] [hiremath-posterior-fossa-2026, p. 3]
- Other morphometry studies (normal mid-trimester BV and brainstem–tentorium angles; hindbrain
  morphometry with choroid plexus position) exist and were not read for this entry.
