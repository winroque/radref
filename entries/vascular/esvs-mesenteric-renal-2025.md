# Mesenteric and renal artery stenosis — ESVS 2025

> The ESVS 2025 guideline replaces the 2017 mesenteric guideline. For imaging it fixes:
> renal artery stenosis (RAS) as **> 50%** narrowing, graded **moderate (50-70%)** and
> **severe (> 70%)** by consensus; chronic mesenteric ischaemia (CMI) as a **clinical** diagnosis
> that needs symptoms plus **> 50% stenosis of the superior mesenteric artery (SMA) and > 50% of
> another mesenteric artery**; duplex first line for chronic mesenteric and renal disease, CTA
> as the preferred anatomical study for CMI, and urgent two-phase CTA for suspected acute
> ischaemia. [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025]

## 1. Scope

- **Applies to:** adults with suspected or known atherosclerotic or non-atherosclerotic disease
  of the coeliac artery (CA), SMA, inferior mesenteric artery (IMA) and renal arteries.
- **Does not apply to / not summarised here:** venous disease (mesenteric and renal veins),
  non-occlusive mesenteric ischaemia, revascularisation technique and drug therapy, children
  (except where a recommendation says so), kidney transplant arteries.

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Renal artery stenosis | > 50% narrowing of at least one renal artery or its branches with impaired flow to the kidney | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 8.1] |
| Moderate / severe RAS | 50-70% / > 70%, "by consensus" | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 8.1] |
| Fibromuscular dysplasia (FMD) | About 10% of RAS; focal or multifocal ("string of beads") stenosis, most often in the middle part of the artery; diagnosed by CTA or MRA | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 8.1] |
| Haemodynamically significant RAS (DSA) | Translesional mean pressure gradient >= 10 mmHg or peak systolic gradient >= 20 mmHg (expert consensus) | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 8.3.3] |
| Chronic mesenteric ischaemia (CMI) | Chronic postprandial pain, food aversion and weight loss with insufficient mucosal perfusion, plus radiological confirmation of chronic mesenteric artery occlusive disease | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 3.2] |
| MALS | Median arcuate ligament syndrome: external compression of the CA | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 4] |

## 3. Criteria / categories

### 3.1 Chronic mesenteric ischaemia — when imaging supports the diagnosis

| Situation | Statement | Class / level | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chronic abdominal symptoms + > 50% SMA stenosis + > 50% stenosis of another mesenteric artery | CMI is recommended to be considered present (Rec. 1) | I C | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025] |
| Chronic abdominal symptoms + isolated SMA stenosis > 70% | CMI may be considered present (Rec. 2) | IIb C | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025] |
| Isolated 50-70% stenosis | Associated with a 0-10% probability of CMI (cited from Terlouw 2020) | text | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 3.2] |

- Symptoms are usually absent until at least two of the three mesenteric arteries are significantly
  stenotic or occluded; a single-vessel mesenteric stenosis is found in up to 18% of the general
  population while the diagnosis is rare. Single-vessel occlusion can cause symptoms, especially
  if the SMA is involved and collaterals are poor. [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 3.2]
- Prediction model (Table 4): weight loss > 5% = 5 points; cardiovascular disease = 2; CA 50-70% =
  4 points, CA > 70% = 11 (MALS: 4 and 9); SMA 50-70% = 4, SMA > 70% = 10. A score of 19 or more
  corroborates the diagnosis (the comparison symbol did not survive text extraction, so confirm "19 or more" on the PDF; the model is from Terlouw 2020). [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 3.2, Table 4]

### 3.2 Imaging methods for CMI

| Method | Statement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Plain abdominal X-ray | No role in diagnosing CMI | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 3.5.1] |
| Duplex ultrasound | First-line exam after at least 4 h fasting (Rec. 4, I B); operator dependent; PSV and EDV higher in expiration than in inspiration; each centre should validate its own thresholds; a stent hinders quantification | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 3.5.2] |
| CTA | Preferred imaging for diagnosis, planning and exclusion of other pathology (Rec. 5, I C); arterial-phase 0.75 mm axial slices with 3D reformatting; shows collateral circulation | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 3.5.3] |
| Contrast-enhanced MRA | May be considered as an alternative to CTA (Rec. 6, IIb C) | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 3.5.4] |
| DSA | Historical reference standard; selective CA, SMA, IMA injections in lateral projection; now mostly confirmatory or therapeutic | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 3.5.5] |

Duplex velocity criteria for mesenteric stenosis (Table 5, selected; validation studies done
fasting; thresholds differ between studies, so the table is a comparison, not a rule):

| Study | SMA PSV >= 50% | SMA PSV >= 70% | CA PSV >= 50% | CA PSV >= 70% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Moneta 1993 (n = 100) | not given | 275 cm/s | not given | 200 cm/s |
| Zwolak 1998 (n = 46) | 300 cm/s | not given | 200 cm/s | not given |
| AbuRahma 2012 (n = 150) | 295 cm/s | 400 cm/s | 240 cm/s | 320 cm/s |
| van Petersen 2013 (n = 324), expiration | 220 cm/s | 268 cm/s | 268 cm/s | 280 cm/s |
| van Petersen 2013, inspiration | 277 cm/s | 205 cm/s | 243 cm/s | 272 cm/s |

End-diastolic velocity thresholds are also tabulated in the source (for example SMA EDV
45 cm/s for >= 50% in Zwolak and AbuRahma). [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, Table 5]

### 3.3 Median arcuate ligament syndrome (MALS)

- CTA pattern that supports MALS: focal narrowing mostly in the expiratory phase, post-stenotic
  dilatation and **absence of atherosclerotic plaque**; always to be confirmed against the clinical
  picture. [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 3.5.3]
- Consensus criteria quoted in Rec. 26 (IIa C, "highly selected" patients): postprandial pain with
  diet modification, unexplained weight loss or diarrhoea; CA stenosis >= 70% from external
  compression shown by **two** imaging techniques including inspiration and expiration CTA with 1 mm
  sections; and normal abdominal ultrasound and upper endoscopy. [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 4]

### 3.4 Acute mesenteric ischaemia — CT protocol

- Urgent CTA with contrast in **arterial and venous phases**, slices <= 1 mm, optional
  non-contrast run, **regardless of renal function** (Rec. 33, **I B**, unchanged from 2017). [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 5.3.4.1]
- The 2025 text says the non-contrast phase is not necessary for the diagnosis (the 2017 guideline
  asked for triphasic CTA); the venous phase is needed for mesenteric venous thrombosis and bowel
  wall assessment. [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 5.3.4.1]

### 3.5 Renal artery stenosis — imaging

| Method | Statement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Duplex ultrasound | First-line imaging in suspected RAS (Rec. 57, I B). Direct criteria: PSV at the stenosis and renal-aortic PSV ratio. Indirect: resistance index (a side-to-side RI difference > 0.05 may be used), waveform shape, delayed acceleration time (tardus-parvus). PSV cut-offs for > 50% stenosis in recent studies: 180-285 cm/s (sensitivity and specificity 70-90%, trading one against the other). RAR > 3.5 indicates > 60% stenosis. Limits: body habitus, bowel gas; FMD in the middle third is hard to see | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 8.3.1] |
| CTA | Sensitivity 64-100% and specificity 62-98% against DSA; meta-analysis (4 studies, 486 patients) 73% / 96% for >= 50% stenosis; shows wall calcification, mural plaque and in-stent stenosis; limited by radiation and contrast risk in CKD | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 8.3.2] |
| Gadolinium MRA | AUC 0.97 (CTA 0.99); meta-analysis 70% / 82% for >= 50% stenosis; non-enhanced MRA sensitivity 97% / specificity 90% at 1.5 T and 96% / 91% at 3.0 T | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 8.3.2] |
| Contrast-enhanced US | May be considered if duplex is inconclusive or CTA/MRA are contraindicated (Rec. 58, IIb C) | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 8.3.1] |
| DSA | Gold standard but invasive; used as confirmatory imaging before intervention | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 8.3.3] |

Algorithm (Fig. 7): duplex first; if no established diagnosis, CTA or enhanced/non-enhanced MRA;
if duplex is inconclusive and CTA/MRA are contraindicated, contrast-enhanced ultrasound.
[esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, Fig. 7]

## 4. Management

| Item | Recommendation | Source |
|---|---|---|
| CMI | Investigate and treat in specialised centres with multidisciplinary assessment (Rec. 3, I C) | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025] |
| Renal artery revascularisation | Angioplasty with or without stenting is **not indicated** in any degree of RAS when hypertension and renal function are controlled (Rec. 63, class III, level A) | [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025, 8.4] |

## 5. Pitfalls

- A mesenteric stenosis is common in people without symptoms; the imaging finding is not the
  diagnosis.
- Mesenteric velocities change with the respiratory phase and with stenoses in the other
  mesenteric arteries; a stent can make duplex unreliable (CTA is then needed).
- Published mesenteric duplex cut-offs come from few and old studies and disagree; the guideline
  gives no single general cut-off.
- CTA specificity for RAS is wide (62-98%); CTA says little about haemodynamics.
- Moderate and severe RAS are consensus labels, not outcome-derived thresholds.

## 6. What the source does not say

- It does not define a "mild" RAS category (< 50% is not RAS by its definition).
- It gives no single validated duplex cut-off for mesenteric stenosis.
- It does not say that a CT finding of >= 2 stenosed mesenteric arteries equals the diagnosis of
  CMI; the diagnosis needs symptoms.
- Recommendations about the IMA, mesenteric venous disease and drug therapy were not summarised.

## 7. Disagreements and newer versions

- Replaces the ESVS 2017 guideline on this topic (cited in the text as "the 2017 ESVS clinical practice guidelines"), which asked for triphasic CTA for acute
  ischaemia; the 2025 text accepts omitting the non-contrast phase.
- ESC 2024 uses a renal duplex PSV of > 200 cm/s for > 50% stenosis and does not grade moderate/severe;
  its quoted CTA specificity range for RAS is 92-98% versus 62-98% in ESVS 2025 [esc-paad-2024].
- ACR Appropriateness Criteria 2018 rate CTA "usually appropriate" for acute and chronic
  mesenteric ischaemia and also MRA for chronic [acr-ac-mesenteric-ischemia-2018].
