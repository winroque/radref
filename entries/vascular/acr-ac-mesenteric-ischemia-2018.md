# Imaging of mesenteric ischaemia — ACR AC 2018

> Which exam to order first when acute or chronic mesenteric ischaemia is suspected.
> **CTA of the abdomen and pelvis with IV contrast is rated "usually appropriate" for both**
> variants; for chronic ischaemia, MRA without and with contrast is also "usually appropriate".
> The document rates appropriateness; it does not define stenosis grades or report wording.
> [acr-ac-mesenteric-ischemia-2018]

## 1. Scope

- **Applies to:** adults with suspected acute (Variant 1) or chronic (Variant 2) mesenteric
  ischaemia, initial imaging.
- **Does not apply to:** treatment, follow-up after revascularisation, children (the document
  notes pediatric dose issues), other causes of abdominal pain beyond differential comments.

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Acute mesenteric ischaemia | Abdominal pain out of proportion to examination; most often SMA embolism (about 40-50% of episodes), then SMA thrombosis (20-30%), non-occlusive ischaemia (about 25%) and venous thrombosis (5-15%) | [acr-ac-mesenteric-ischemia-2018, Pathophysiology] |
| Chronic mesenteric ischaemia | Almost always severe atherosclerosis; typically occlusion or severe stenosis of at least two of the main mesenteric vessels | [acr-ac-mesenteric-ischemia-2018, abstract] |
| CTA (ACR usage) | Thin-section CT timed to peak arterial or venous enhancement, read with multiplanar and **3-D** reconstructions; 3-D rendering is the element that separates CTA from routine contrast CT | [acr-ac-mesenteric-ischemia-2018, CTA] |

## 3. Criteria / categories

Ratings as printed in Variants 1 and 2 (Usually Appropriate = 7-9; May Be Appropriate = 4-6;
Usually Not Appropriate = 1-3; "Disagreement" = the panel's ratings were dispersed).

| Procedure | Variant 1: suspected acute | Variant 2: suspected chronic |
|---|---|---|
| CTA abdomen and pelvis with IV contrast | Usually appropriate | Usually appropriate |
| MRA abdomen and pelvis without and with IV contrast | May be appropriate (disagreement) | **Usually appropriate** |
| CT abdomen and pelvis with IV contrast | May be appropriate | May be appropriate |
| Arteriography abdomen | May be appropriate (disagreement) | May be appropriate (disagreement) |
| US duplex Doppler abdomen | May be appropriate | May be appropriate |
| MRA without IV contrast | Usually not appropriate | May be appropriate |
| Radiography abdomen | May be appropriate | Usually not appropriate |
| CT without IV contrast | Usually not appropriate | Usually not appropriate |
| CT without and with IV contrast | Usually not appropriate | Usually not appropriate |

[acr-ac-mesenteric-ischemia-2018, Variants 1 and 2]

Points from the discussion:
- Acute: arterial and portal venous phases are both part of the CTA protocol; a non-contrast phase
  may help (intramural haemorrhage, calcification, baseline for enhancement) but several studies
  found it not required; CTA is preferred even with GFR below 30 because the benefit of a fast
  diagnosis usually outweighs contrast risk; reported CTA sensitivity and specificity as high as
  93-100%. Radiographs may be normal in 25% of acute cases. [acr-ac-mesenteric-ischemia-2018, Variant 1]
- Contrast CT in the venous phase alone can miss arterial lesions; arterial phase changed care in
  19% of patients in one study cited. [acr-ac-mesenteric-ischemia-2018, CT]
- Arteriography was the reference standard (sensitivity 74-100%, specificity 100% in the studies
  cited) and allows therapy. [acr-ac-mesenteric-ischemia-2018, Arteriography]

## 4. Management

The document is an imaging-selection guide. It states that CTA helps triage who needs angiography
versus emergent surgery, and that early angiography is linked to survival, while noting
controversy on angiography before surgery when peritoneal signs are present.
[acr-ac-mesenteric-ischemia-2018]

## 5. Pitfalls

- Ratings depend on the clinical scenario (suspected acute versus suspected chronic); the table
  is not a diagnostic criterion.
- Two ratings carry "disagreement" flags (arteriography; MRA for acute).
- Ratings are from 2018; later guidelines may differ (see 7).

## 6. What the source does not say

- No stenosis percentages or velocity thresholds.
- No CT findings checklist for bowel ischaemia beyond the list in the discussion (wall thickening,
  hypoattenuation, dilatation, fat stranding, pneumatosis, portal venous gas).
- No wording for the report.

## 7. Disagreements and newer versions

- ESVS 2025 recommends duplex first for chronic ischaemia (Rec. 4) and CTA as the preferred
  anatomical exam; ACR AC 2018 rates CTA and MRA "usually appropriate" and duplex only "may be
  appropriate" [esvs-mesenteric-renal-2025].
- Check whether ACR has issued a newer revision of this topic before using the ratings; this entry
  was not checked against the ACR website.
