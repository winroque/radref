# Abdominal aortic aneurysm — surveillance (SVS 2018, ESVS 2024)

> An AAA is a focal abdominal aortic diameter of **≥ 3.0 cm**. Ultrasound is the first-line
> method for diagnosis and surveillance. Intervals grow shorter with diameter; ESVS 2024 is
> sex-specific and shortens the 6-month interval to **≥ 4.5 cm in women**. The caliper method
> (outer-to-outer, leading-edge, inner-to-inner) changes the number by several millimetres, so
> it must be stated and kept constant. [svs-aaa-2018; esvs-aaa-2024]

## 1. Scope

- **Applies to:** infrarenal AAA and sub-aneurysmal dilatation found at US or CT in adults.
- **Does not cover:** repair technique, post-EVAR surveillance, thoracic aorta.

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| AAA | Enlargement of the abdominal aorta to ≥ 3.0 cm | [svs-aaa-2018] |
| Aneurysm (general) | Permanent focal dilatation ≥ 50% above the expected normal diameter; in practice a fixed ≥ 30 mm threshold is used for the abdominal aorta (a lower threshold may suit women and some Asian populations) | [esvs-aaa-2024] |
| Sub-aneurysmal aorta | 25–29 mm (ESVS surveillance category) | [esvs-aaa-2024] |
| Caliper methods | OTO (outer to outer), LELE (leading edge to leading edge), ITI (inner to inner) | [esvs-aaa-2024, Fig. 1] |

## 3. Criteria / categories

### Measurement

- SVS: report the maximum transverse diameter orthogonal to the vessel axis; at CT, outer wall to
  outer wall perpendicular to the aortic path (good practice statement). Ultrasound preferred for
  screening and surveillance (1A). [svs-aaa-2018]
- ESVS: measure perpendicular to the longitudinal axis; the **anteroposterior** plane with a
  consistent caliper placement is the preferred US method (Rec. 8, IIa B). No caliper method is
  recommended over another; use one consistently and state it. ITI is about 3–6 mm smaller than
  OTO, LELE in between; compared with ITI, AAA prevalence rises 31% with LELE and 77% with OTO.
  Systolic vs diastolic measurement differs by about 2 mm. [esvs-aaa-2024]

### Surveillance intervals

| Diameter | SVS 2018 (sex-neutral) | ESVS 2024 men (Rec. 13) | ESVS 2024 women (Rec. 14) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.5–2.9 cm | Rescreen after 10 years (if > 2.5 and < 3.0 cm at screening) | Every 5 years | Every 5 years |
| 3.0–3.9 cm | Every 3 years | Every 3 years | Every 3 years |
| 4.0–4.4 cm | Every 12 months | Annually | Annually |
| 4.5–4.9 cm | Every 12 months | Annually | **Every 6 months** |
| 5.0–5.4 cm | Every 6 months | Every 6 months | Every 6 months |

Sources: [svs-aaa-2018] (all weak recommendations, low-quality evidence); [esvs-aaa-2024]
(Class IIa; intervals adjusted for life expectancy, fitness for repair and patient preference).

### Repair thresholds (for context)

| Population | SVS 2018 | ESVS 2024 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Men, asymptomatic fusiform | Elective repair ≥ 5.5 cm (1A) | Not recommended < 55 mm; consider ≥ 55 mm (IIa C) | [svs-aaa-2018; esvs-aaa-2024] |
| Women | Suggest repair at 5.0–5.4 cm (2B) | Not recommended < 50 mm; threshold to consider 50 mm | [svs-aaa-2018; esvs-aaa-2024] |
| Saccular, symptomatic | Suggest repair of saccular AAA; repair if pain attributable to AAA | — | [svs-aaa-2018] |

ESVS 2024 states the diameter threshold for considering repair should preferably be based on the
US measurement. [esvs-aaa-2024]

## 4. Management

- Report: maximum diameter, plane (AP), caliper method, sex-specific interval.
- Refer to a vascular surgeon at initial diagnosis (SVS good practice statement).
  [svs-aaa-2018]
- Iliac artery aneurysm (ESVS Rec. 134): US surveillance every 3 years at 20–24 mm, every 2 years
  at 25–29 mm, yearly at ≥ 30 mm. [esvs-aaa-2024]
- Suspected AAA with recent abdominal or back pain → CT (SVS 1B). [svs-aaa-2018]

## 5. Pitfalls

- Mixing caliper methods between exams (a 3–6-mm swing can cross a threshold).
- Measuring an oblique axial section of a tortuous aorta (overestimates the diameter).
- Comparing a CT diameter with an earlier US diameter without saying so.

## 6. What the source does not say

- Neither guideline makes a measurement threshold specific to Brazilian populations.
- SVS does not give sex-specific surveillance intervals.

## 7. Disagreements and newer versions

- SVS (2018) and ESVS (2024) disagree on the sub-aneurysmal interval (10 vs 5 years) and on
  sex-specific intervals (ESVS shortens to 6 months at ≥ 45 mm in women).
- SVS recommends OTO at CT; ESVS declines to prefer any US caliper method.

## 8. How to cite

- `esvs-aaa-2024` for intervals by sex; `svs-aaa-2018` for the US definition and SVS intervals.
