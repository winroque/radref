# Incidental vascular findings on abdominal/pelvic CT and MRI (ACR white paper, 2013)

> Recommendations for incidental arterial and venous findings on abdominal and pelvic CT/MRI:
> abdominal aortic diameter and first follow-up interval, visceral and iliac aneurysms, and
> common venous variants. Many recommendations depend on comorbidity and growth rate. Guidance,
> not a standard of care. [acr-vascular-2013]

## 1. Scope

- **Applies to:** incidental vascular findings on abdominal/pelvic CT or MRI.
- **Out of scope:** aortic dissection and intramural haematoma (almost always symptomatic).
  [acr-vascular-2013]

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Normal abdominal aorta | Suprarenal up to **3.0 cm**; infrarenal **2.0 cm** | [acr-vascular-2013] |
| Abdominal aortic aneurysm (infrarenal) | **≥ 3.0 cm** or **≥ 1.5×** normal diameter (9% of people > 65 years by these criteria) | [acr-vascular-2013] |
| Iliac aneurysm | **≥ 1.5×** normal or **≥ 2.5 cm** | [acr-vascular-2013] |

## 3. Criteria / categories

### Abdominal aorta — interval to the **first** follow-up

| Diameter | Interval | Source |
|---|---|---|
| < 2.5 cm | No follow-up | [acr-vascular-2013] |
| 2.5–2.9 cm (ectasia) | 5 years | [acr-vascular-2013] |
| 3.0–3.4 cm | 3 years | [acr-vascular-2013] |
| 3.5–3.9 cm | 2 years | [acr-vascular-2013] |
| 4.0–4.4 cm | 1 year | [acr-vascular-2013] |
| 4.5–4.9 cm | 6 months + consider surgical/endovascular referral | [acr-vascular-2013] |
| 5.0–5.5 cm | 3–6 months + consider referral | [acr-vascular-2013] |

(Table 1 is headed "Aortic diameter (mm)" but the values are in cm.) [acr-vascular-2013, Table 1]

Adjust for comorbidity and growth. Asymptomatic penetrating aortic ulcer: **yearly** imaging
(more often if symptoms), consider intervention. [acr-vascular-2013]

### Arterial aneurysms

| Territory | Recommendation | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Iliac | < 3.0 cm: rarely ruptures, grows slowly (no interval given). **3.0–3.5 cm**: cross-sectional imaging at ~**6 months**, then **yearly** if stable. **> 3.5 cm**: closer follow-up or treatment | [acr-vascular-2013] |
| Splenic | **≥ 2 cm** → consider endovascular treatment; smaller → **yearly** follow-up. Higher risk: rapid growth, women of childbearing age, cirrhosis, symptoms | [acr-vascular-2013] |
| Renal | **1.0–1.5 cm** → follow-up every **1–2 years**; **> 1.5–2.0 cm** → consider repair. One cited proposal: repair all ≥ 1 cm with uncontrolled hypertension. Consider fibromuscular dysplasia | [acr-vascular-2013] |
| Other visceral (hepatic, coeliac, mesenteric, gastroduodenal) | Usually treat if **> 2 cm** (lower threshold if non-atherosclerotic); if observed, **yearly** (first may be sooner, especially pseudoaneurysm) | [acr-vascular-2013] |
| Pancreaticoduodenal | Higher rupture risk: consider treatment **at any size** | [acr-vascular-2013] |

### Other arterial findings

- Isolated visceral artery dissection (usually SMA), asymptomatic: imaging follow-up rather
  than immediate treatment (no interval defined).
- Coeliac compression by the median arcuate ligament without symptoms: nothing.
- Atherosclerotic mesenteric ostial stenosis compensated by collaterals, without intestinal
  angina/weight loss: nothing. [acr-vascular-2013]

### Venous findings

- IVC anomalies (duplication, left IVC, azygos continuation, retroaortic/circumaortic left
  renal vein): no action; relevant for donors and procedures.
- IVC pseudothrombus: laminar flow of contrast-laden blood from the renal veins beside unenhanced
  blood from the legs; the most common filling defect (mixing/reflux produce similar defects); delayed phase, US
  or MR angiography if indeterminate. Reflux into IVC/hepatic veins may occur with injection
  > 3 mL/s.
- May-Thurner compression (~25% of the population): no follow-up unless unilateral oedema or
  thrombosis.
- Nutcracker (left renal vein between aorta and SMA): without hypertension, proteinuria or
  haematuria, probably unimportant.
- Chronic venous thrombosis: usually no imaging follow-up.
- Chronic thrombosis criteria: small vein, wall thickening, extensive collaterals, no filling
  defect other than webs. [acr-vascular-2013]
- Dilated gonadal/pelvic veins in asymptomatic women: no further imaging or intervention.
  Ovarian veins > 8 mm with incompetent valves are common in asymptomatic multiparous women
  (dilated ovarian veins in 47% of asymptomatic potential renal donors). Gonadal vein
  thrombosis is seen in up to 80% of asymptomatic women on routine CT after hysterectomy and
  lymphadenectomy for neoplasm. [acr-vascular-2013]

## 5. Pitfalls

- Table 1 unit label says mm; values are cm.
- Ambiguities in the text (not resolved): for splenic aneurysms the paper says that "among the
  smaller of those ≥ 2 cm" intervals > 1 year may be reasonable — probably meaning those
  < 2 cm. The take-home points attribute the ≥ 2 cm cut-off to the **renal** artery, while the
  body gives it for the **splenic** artery and > 1.5–2.0 cm for the renal. The body of the
  text is the more consistent reading. The AAA take-home point also says "endoscopic" referral
  where the body says endovascular. [acr-vascular-2013]

## 6. What the source does not say

- The aortic table gives only the **first** follow-up interval, not a full surveillance
  programme.

## 7. Disagreements and newer versions

- AAA surveillance intervals differ in newer guidelines: SVS 2018, ESVS 2024 (sex-specific),
  and the Brazilian DIC/SBC 2019 statement (`doppler-br-2019-territories`); see `aaa-surveillance-svs-esvs`.
