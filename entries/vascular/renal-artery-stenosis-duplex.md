# Renal artery stenosis on duplex ultrasound

> Duplex of the native renal arteries combines **direct** criteria at the stenosis (peak
> systolic velocity, PSV; renal–aortic ratio, RAR) with **intrarenal** criteria downstream
> (acceleration time, acceleration index, loss of the early systolic peak). There is no single
> agreed threshold: the classic cut-offs (PSV 180–200 cm/s, RAR 3.5) come from small
> angiographic series of the 1980s–1990s, and a meta-analysis found PSV the most accurate single
> parameter (sensitivity 85%, specificity 92%). [williams-renal-duplex-2007]
> [hoffmann-renal-duplex-1991] [kohler-renal-duplex-1986]

## 1. Scope

- **Applies to:** adults with suspected atherosclerotic renal artery stenosis (RAS) or
  follow-up after angioplasty, stent or bypass. [aium-renal-vessels-2020]
  [acr-renal-vessels-pp-2023]
- **Imaging choice:** duplex ultrasound is "usually appropriate" as initial imaging for
  suspected renovascular hypertension with normal or decreased renal function (eGFR
  < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²), for follow-up of known RAS and after treatment; MRA with and without
  contrast is also usually appropriate in all five variants, and CTA in variants 1, 3 and 5
  (normal renal function or post-treatment); CTA is usually not appropriate when eGFR is
  < 30 mL/min/1.73 m². [acr-ac-renovascular-2026, variants 1–5]
- **Does not apply to:** fibromuscular dysplasia (FMD) as a first test — the FMD consensus
  makes CTA the initial test and reserves duplex as the first test for specialised centres;
  kidney transplants (see `renal-transplant-doppler`); children (the practice parameters
  accept a single PSV in the main renal artery in infants and young children). [gornik-fmd-2019]
  [acr-renal-vessels-pp-2023]

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| PSV | Highest angle-corrected peak systolic velocity in the main renal artery, recorded at least at origin/proximal, mid and hilar segments and at any site of aliasing or narrowing | [acr-renal-vessels-pp-2023] [aium-renal-vessels-2020] |
| RAR | PSV in the stenosis divided by aortic PSV recorded at or slightly cephalad to the renal artery origins | [acr-renal-vessels-pp-2023] [kohler-renal-duplex-1986] |
| Acceleration time (AT) | Time from the start of systole to the early systolic peak (or to the point of slope change), in seconds | [stavros-tardus-parvus-1992] |
| Acceleration index (AI) | Slope of the early systolic upstroke, m/s² (needs angle correction, ideally ≤ 30°) | [stavros-tardus-parvus-1992] [acr-renal-vessels-pp-2023] |
| Early systolic peak (ESP) | Early systolic compliance peak / reflective-wave complex; its loss is the qualitative tardus–parvus sign | [stavros-tardus-parvus-1992] |
| Tardus / parvus | Delayed systolic acceleration / reduced, rounded systolic peak distal to a significant stenosis | [stavros-tardus-parvus-1992] |
| Technique limits | Angle ≤ 60° (as small as feasible); report any segment not seen (e.g. the origin); look for accessory arteries; renal length always measured; intrarenal spectra from upper, mid and lower kidney with fast sweep and optimised scale | [acr-renal-vessels-pp-2023] [aium-renal-vessels-2020] |

## 3. Criteria / categories

### 3.1 Direct criteria in the original series

| Study | Reference standard | Criterion | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kohler 1986, retrospective, 43 arteries | Angiographic stenosis > 60% | RAR > 3.5 | Sensitivity 91% (20/22), specificity 95% (20/21); 1 of 4 occlusions missed (collateral taken for the renal artery) | [kohler-renal-duplex-1986] |
| Hoffmann 1991, 85 arteries / 41 patients | Any focal lesion ("diseased" artery) | PSV > 180 cm/s | Sensitivity 95%, specificity 90%; mean PSV 138 ± 63 (normal arteries) vs 370 ± 149 cm/s (diseased); normal volunteers 104 ± 25 cm/s | [hoffmann-renal-duplex-1991] |
| Hoffmann 1991 | Stenosis > 60% | RAR > 3.5 | Sensitivity 92% (44/48), specificity 62% (16/26; 9 of the 10 "false positives" were 50–60% stenoses) | [hoffmann-renal-duplex-1991, Table 3] |
| Olin 1995, prospective, 187 arteries / 102 patients | Stenosis ≥ 60% | RAR ≥ 3.5 or PSV > 200 cm/s (either) | Sensitivity 98%, specificity 98%, PPV 99%, NPV 97% | [olin-renal-duplex-1995, Table 1] |
| Olin 1995 | Stenosis 80–99% | End-diastolic velocity ≥ 150 cm/s | 81% (38/47) of arteries with EDV ≥ 150 cm/s had 80–99% stenosis | [olin-renal-duplex-1995] |
| Zachrisson 2017, 76 arteries | Translesional peak systolic gradient ≥ 15 mmHg | PSV ≥ 180 and RAR ≥ 3.5 | Sensitivity 62%, specificity 91% | [zachrisson-renal-duplex-2017, Table 1] |
| Zachrisson 2017 | Same | RAR ≥ 2.6 alone | Sensitivity 89%, specificity 69% (proposed for screening) | [zachrisson-renal-duplex-2017, Table 1] |

Olin's mean values: RAR 2.1 ± 0.8 (0–59%), 5.1 ± 0.6 (60–79%), 6.1 ± 1.9 (80–99%); PSV 146 ± 49,
363 ± 101 and 380 ± 103 cm/s in the same groups. PSV and RAR did not usefully separate 60–79%
from 80–99%. Occlusion: no flow in the renal artery and a low-amplitude parenchymal signal.
[olin-renal-duplex-1995]

### 3.2 Pooled accuracy (meta-analysis of 88 studies, 9,974 arteries)

| Parameter (thresholds used in the studies) | Sensitivity | Specificity | Diagnostic OR | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PSV (100–200 cm/s), 21 studies | 85% (76–90) | 92% | 60.9 | [williams-renal-duplex-2007, Table 2] |
| RAR (1.8–3.5), 13 studies | 78% (67–86) | 89% | 29.3 | [williams-renal-duplex-2007, Table 2] |
| AT, 13 studies | 80% (62–91) | 88% | 28.9 | [williams-renal-duplex-2007, Table 2] |
| AI (3–4.5 m/s²), 13 studies | 74% (55–87) | 85% | 16.0 | [williams-renal-duplex-2007, Table 2] |

In paired studies PSV beat RAR (relative diagnostic OR 1.8) and AI (5.3); adding other
parameters to PSV did not clearly improve accuracy (relative diagnostic OR 1.6, p = 0.09).
Specificity is 1 − the "1 − specificity" values of Table 2. [williams-renal-duplex-2007]

### 3.3 Intrarenal (indirect) criteria

| Criterion (segmental arteries, stenosis ≥ 60%, prevalence 30%) | Sensitivity | Specificity | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| AT ≥ 0.07 s | 78% | 94% | [stavros-tardus-parvus-1992, Table 2] |
| AI < 3.0 m/s² | 89% | 83% | [stavros-tardus-parvus-1992, Table 2] |
| Loss of ESP (pattern recognition) | 95% | 97% | [stavros-tardus-parvus-1992, Table 2] |

- Kliewer 1993 (distal renal artery, stenosis > 50%): no parameter separated stenotic from
  non-stenotic arteries; AT and systolic acceleration differed only for 80–95% stenosis; the
  authors concluded tardus–parvus is **not an adequate screening method**.
  [kliewer-tardus-parvus-1993]
- EFSUMB: normal AT < 0.07 s and AI > 3 m/s²; thresholds of 0.09–0.12 s raise specificity;
  tardus–parvus is seen mainly with stenoses above 70–80% and may be absent when vessels are
  stiff. [efsumb-renal-vessels-2024]

### 3.4 Stents

| Study | Criterion for > 50% in-stent restenosis | Sensitivity / specificity | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bakker 1999 (33 stents, 23 assessable, 4 restenoses assessable) | In-stent PSV > 226 cm/s | 100% / 90% | [bakker-renal-stent-1999] |
| Same | RAR > 2.7 | 100% / 84% | [bakker-renal-stent-1999] |
| Same, native-artery criteria | PSV > 180 cm/s; RAR > 3.5 | 100% / 74%; 50% / 89% | [bakker-renal-stent-1999] |
| DIC/SBC 2019 | Stent PSV ≥ 390 cm/s and RAR ≥ 5 for ≥ 60% | — | [doppler-br-2019, Table 15] |

Record PSV proximal to, within and distal to the stent. [acr-renal-vessels-pp-2023]

## 4. Management

- The sources grade stenosis; none of them tells the radiologist when to treat. The ACR
  Appropriateness Criteria note that the CORAL trial found no benefit of stenting over medical
  therapy for atherosclerotic RAS with hypertension or CKD, so testing is not usually warranted
  when hypertension is well controlled. [acr-ac-renovascular-2026]
- Equivocal duplex: CTA (high negative predictive value) or MRA. [efsumb-renal-vessels-2024]
  [acr-ac-renovascular-2026]
- FMD: CTA first; multifocal FMD needs translesional pressure gradients to judge
  haemodynamic significance. [gornik-fmd-2019]
- Resistive index as a predictor of benefit from revascularisation: see
  `renal-resistive-index`.

## 5. Pitfalls

- **What the cut-off was validated for.** Hoffmann's 180 cm/s identifies an abnormal artery
  (any focal lesion), not a ≥ 60% stenosis; RAR 3.5 was the ≥ 60% criterion. Kohler and
  Hoffmann both used angiographic > 60%. [hoffmann-renal-duplex-1991]
  [kohler-renal-duplex-1986]
- **Aorta:** aortic PSV varies with age (Kohler: r = −0.37) and is unreliable with aortic
  disease; the DIC/SBC statement lists aortic PSV > 100 or < 40 cm/s, coarctation, severe LV
  dysfunction, dissection and aneurysm as conditions that distort the ratio.
  [kohler-renal-duplex-1986] [doppler-br-2019, §3.4]
- **Accessory arteries** are usually missed (Hoffmann 0/7; Zachrisson 0/13); EFSUMB cites an
  isolated significant accessory-artery stenosis rate of 1.5%. [hoffmann-renal-duplex-1991]
  [zachrisson-renal-duplex-2017] [efsumb-renal-vessels-2024]
- **Technically inadequate studies:** about 10% (Kohler) and 13% (Hoffmann); Olin repeated the scan
  rather than report an incomplete study; the meta-analysis found failure rates of 0–54%.
  [kohler-renal-duplex-1986] [olin-renal-duplex-1995] [williams-renal-duplex-2007]
- **False tardus–parvus** from too high a velocity scale, low Doppler power or a deep anterior
  window (Stavros' checklist); tardus–parvus cannot tell occlusion with collaterals from
  ≥ 80% stenosis. [stavros-tardus-parvus-1992]
- **Small and selected samples:** all single-centre; prevalence 30–90%; the stent cut-offs rest
  on 4 assessable restenoses. [williams-renal-duplex-2007] [bakker-renal-stent-1999]
- Bakker concluded that duplex is sensitive for in-stent restenosis only with
  laboratory-specific thresholds. [bakker-renal-stent-1999]

## 6. What the source does not say

- **The AIUM and ACR practice parameters give no stenosis thresholds.** They define what to
  record (PSV sites, aortic PSV, RAR, intrarenal AT/AI/RI and waveform shape), not cut-offs.
  [aium-renal-vessels-2020] [acr-renal-vessels-pp-2023]
- **The ACR Appropriateness Criteria do not adopt a threshold:** they quote PSV cut-offs of
  180–300 cm/s from different studies and call RAR 3.5 "commonly reported".
  [acr-ac-renovascular-2026]
- **ESC 2024 attaches "sensitivity 95%, specificity 90%" to PSV > 200 cm/s for > 50% RAS;** the
  same pair of figures is what Hoffmann reported for PSV > 180 cm/s detecting any focal lesion.
  This entry did not check which study ESC cites for it. [esc-paad-2024]
  [hoffmann-renal-duplex-1991]
- **No source here grades 50–59% vs 60–79% by duplex alone;** Olin found PSV and RAR overlapping
  between 60–79% and 80–99%. [olin-renal-duplex-1995]

## 7. Disagreements and newer versions

- PSV threshold: 180 (Hoffmann), 200 (Olin; DIC/SBC), 300 cm/s for specificity (cited by the
  ACR AC). RAR: 3.5 (Kohler, Hoffmann, Olin, DIC/SBC) vs 2.6 for screening (Zachrisson).
  [hoffmann-renal-duplex-1991] [olin-renal-duplex-1995] [doppler-br-2019, Table 15]
  [acr-ac-renovascular-2026] [zachrisson-renal-duplex-2017]
- Intrarenal criteria: favourable (Stavros) vs unfavourable (Kliewer).
  [stavros-tardus-parvus-1992] [kliewer-tardus-parvus-1993]
- Side-to-side RI difference as an extra criterion: ESC prints "≥ 0.5", ESVS "> 0.05" (see
  `esc-paad-2024`).
- Brazilian validation studies (Engelhorn 2004 and 2005; Cardoso 2006; Borelli 2013) found
  optimal cut-offs from PSV 189 to 250 cm/s and RAR 2.6 to 3.2; they were not read in full for
  this entry.
