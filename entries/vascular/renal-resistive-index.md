# Renal resistive index (RI)

> RI = (peak systolic velocity − end-diastolic velocity) / peak systolic velocity, measured in
> segmental, interlobar or arcuate arteries. Normal mean is about 0.60 and 0.70 is the usual
> upper limit in adults. RI is a non-specific marker: it depends on vascular compliance, pulse
> pressure and heart rate as well as on renal vascular resistance. [tublin-ri-2003]
> [viazzi-ri-2014]

## 1. Scope

- **Applies to:** intrarenal arterial spectra in native kidneys and transplants.
- **Does not apply to:** diagnosis of renal artery stenosis by RI alone (the sources use
  tardus–parvus and direct velocities for that; see `renal-artery-stenosis-duplex`).
  [viazzi-ri-2014]

## 2. Definitions and technique

| Item | Statement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Formula | (PSV − EDV) / PSV; Radermacher reports it ×100 (an "RI of 80" = 0.80) | [tublin-ri-2003] [radermacher-ri-ras-2001] |
| Vessels | Arcuate (corticomedullary junction) or interlobar arteries, 2–4 mm gate; Radermacher used segmental arteries | [tublin-ri-2003] [radermacher-ri-ras-2001] |
| Settings | Lowest PRF without aliasing, highest gain without noise, lowest wall filter | [tublin-ri-2003] [viazzi-ri-2014] |
| Sampling | 3–5 reproducible waveforms per kidney, averaged (Viazzi: mean of three) | [tublin-ri-2003] [viazzi-ri-2014] |
| Angle | RI and AT do not need angle correction | [efsumb-renal-vessels-2024] |
| Reproducibility | Coefficient of variation 2.0–3.2% (Radermacher 2001) | [radermacher-ri-ras-2001] |

## 3. Criteria / reference values

| Setting | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Normal adults | Mean about 0.60 (reported 0.58–0.64); upper limit 0.70 | [tublin-ri-2003] [viazzi-ri-2014] [efsumb-renal-vessels-2024] |
| Children | Mean RI often > 0.70 in the first year and can exceed 0.70 up to at least 4 years (Tublin); higher in the first 2 years, adult-like between 2 and 6 years, adult threshold not to be used under 6 years (EFSUMB) | [tublin-ri-2003] [efsumb-renal-vessels-2024] |
| Older adults | Can exceed 0.70 without renal insufficiency; EFSUMB gives 10–15% higher values than in younger adults | [tublin-ri-2003] [efsumb-renal-vessels-2024] |
| Obstruction (Platt 1989, 21 kidneys before nephrostomy) | RI > 0.70 in 13/14 obstructed vs 0/7 non-obstructed; fell to ≤ 0.70 in 9/10 after drainage | [platt-ri-obstruction-1989] |
| Obstruction, side-to-side | Difference > 0.10 seen only with true obstruction (Platt series cited by Tublin); EFSUMB uses ≥ 0.08 | [tublin-ri-2003] [efsumb-renal-vessels-2024] |
| Acute tubular necrosis | RI > 0.75 in more than 90% (pre-renal failure usually normal) | [efsumb-renal-vessels-2024] |
| RAS before revascularisation | RI ≥ 0.80 (both kidneys, segmental arteries) predicted no benefit (see §4) | [radermacher-ri-ras-2001] |
| Transplant ≥ 3 months | RI ≥ 0.80 associated with graft loss and death (see §4) | [radermacher-ri-transplant-2003] |

## 4. Management (what the studies found; none sets a treatment rule)

- **Revascularisation (Radermacher 2001):** of 131 patients with > 50% RAS treated
  successfully, the 35 with RI ≥ 0.80 had no fall in mean arterial pressure of ≥ 10 mmHg in 34
  (97%), worsening renal function in 28 (80%), dialysis in 16 (46%) and death in 10 (29%) over
  32 ± 21 months; among the 96 with RI < 0.80, renal function worsened in 3 (3%) and 3 died.
  [radermacher-ri-ras-2001]
- **Later evidence is mixed:** the ACR AC 2026 reports that other studies did not confirm a
  difference in outcome by RI and argued against using an elevated RI as a contraindication.
  [acr-ac-renovascular-2026]
- **Transplant (Radermacher 2003, 601 recipients ≥ 3 months after transplantation):** 122 (20%)
  had RI ≥ 0.80; combined end point (≥ 50% fall in creatinine clearance, dialysis or death) in
  88% vs 17%; multivariate relative risk 9.1 (6.6–12.7). Dialysis 47% vs 9%; death 30% vs 7%.
  [radermacher-ri-transplant-2003]
- **Olin 1995:** among 80–99% stenoses, 79% of arteries with EDV ≥ 150 cm/s had RI < 0.70,
  while 93% of those with EDV < 150 cm/s had RI ≥ 0.70 — a high intrarenal resistance can
  blunt the diastolic jet. [olin-renal-duplex-1995]

## 5. Pitfalls

- **Compliance, not only resistance.** In vitro and ex vivo work showed RI depends on vascular
  compliance, becomes independent of resistance as compliance falls, and tracks the pulse
  pressure index; RI rose only with marked, likely non-physiological, vasoconstriction.
  [tublin-ri-2003]
- **Heart rate:** a pulse below 50/min may raise RI and above 70/min may lower it; the Valsalva
  manoeuvre and the measurement site also change it. [radermacher-ri-transplant-2003]
- **Partial obstruction:** sensitivity as low as 52% (Chen, cited by Tublin); renal colic
  series reported 44% sensitivity / 82% specificity; NSAIDs, hydration and contrast may
  confound. [tublin-ri-2003]
- **Renal disease type:** early biopsy correlation (normal RI in glomerular disease, high in
  vascular/interstitial disease) was not reproduced. [tublin-ri-2003]
- **Transplants:** an elevated RI is non-specific for rejection, ATN and drug toxicity (Perrella:
  sensitivity 43%, specificity 67% at RI 0.90); Radermacher excluded patients with ATN,
  rejection, untreated stenosis, hydronephrosis and compression. [tublin-ri-2003]
  [radermacher-ri-transplant-2003]
- **Renal vein thrombosis** raises RI (reversed diastolic flow in transplants).
  [efsumb-renal-vessels-2024]

## 6. What the source does not say

- **No source here makes RI ≥ 0.80 a contraindication to treatment.** Radermacher reported
  outcomes; the "contraindication" wording appears in Viazzi's review, and the ACR AC 2026
  cites studies that argue against it. [radermacher-ri-ras-2001] [viazzi-ri-2014]
  [acr-ac-renovascular-2026]
- **Radermacher 2003 did not show that RI tracks histology** — RI was not correlated with
  6-month biopsy findings. [radermacher-ri-transplant-2003]
- **Age-specific adult cut-offs** are not given; the sources only state that older adults may
  exceed 0.70. [tublin-ri-2003] [efsumb-renal-vessels-2024]

## 7. Disagreements and newer versions

- The ACR AC renal transplant 2024 states that in Radermacher 2003, 47% of patients with RI
  > 0.80 "developed chronic allograft nephropathy" vs 9%; in the article itself 47% vs 9% is
  the proportion needing **dialysis**. [acr-ac-transplant-2024]
  [radermacher-ri-transplant-2003]
- Side-to-side RI difference for obstruction: > 0.10 (Platt series) vs ≥ 0.08 (EFSUMB); for
  renal artery stenosis the ESC and ESVS print different thresholds (see `esc-paad-2024`).
  [tublin-ri-2003] [efsumb-renal-vessels-2024]
