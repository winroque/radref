# RECIST 1.1 (2009)

> Measurable lesion: **≥ 10 mm** longest diameter at CT (slice ≤ 5 mm); lymph node **≥ 15 mm
> short axis**. Up to **5 target lesions** (≤ 2 per organ). Response by the sum of diameters
> (long axis for non-nodal, short axis for nodes): PR ≥ 30% decrease from baseline; PD ≥ 20%
> increase from the nadir **and** ≥ 5 mm absolute, or any unequivocal new lesion.
> [recist-1-1-2009]

## 1. Scope

- **Applies to:** solid-tumour response assessment in clinical trials; it may be applied to brain
  tumours, although specific criteria exist for them. The authors state it is not meant to
  guide routine clinical decisions unless the oncologist judges it appropriate; in practice it
  is widely reused in oncologic follow-up reports. [recist-1-1-2009, §2]
- **Does not apply to:** lymphoma (other criteria). [recist-1-1-2009] (Immunotherapy response
  patterns are a later issue, handled by iRECIST, not by this 2009 source.)

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Measurable tumour lesion | Longest diameter ≥ 10 mm at CT (slice thickness ≤ 5 mm), ≥ 10 mm by caliper at clinical exam, ≥ 20 mm on chest X-ray | [recist-1-1-2009] |
| Measurable (pathological) node | Short axis ≥ 15 mm at CT; only the short axis is recorded and followed | [recist-1-1-2009] |
| Pathological non-measurable node | Short axis ≥ 10 and < 15 mm (non-target) | [recist-1-1-2009] |
| Normal node | Short axis < 10 mm: non-pathological, not recorded or followed at baseline (a target node that shrinks below 10 mm keeps being measured) | [recist-1-1-2009] |
| Non-measurable lesions | Lesions < 10 mm (or nodes 10–< 15 mm) and truly non-measurable disease: leptomeningeal disease, ascites, pleural/pericardial effusion, inflammatory breast disease, lymphangitic involvement of skin or lung, masses only palpable | [recist-1-1-2009, §3] |
| Previously irradiated or locally treated lesions | Usually non-measurable unless progression has been shown | [recist-1-1-2009, §3] |
| Bone | Lytic or mixed lesions with a measurable soft-tissue component can be measurable; blastic lesions are non-measurable; bone scan, PET and plain films do not measure bone lesions | [recist-1-1-2009] |
| Cystic lesions | Simple cysts are not malignant lesions; cystic metastases may be measurable, but non-cystic lesions are preferred as targets | [recist-1-1-2009] |
| Target lesions | Up to 5 in total, ≤ 2 per organ, chosen for size and reproducibility, representative of involved organs | [recist-1-1-2009] |
| Baseline timing | Scans as close as possible to treatment start, never more than 4 weeks before | [recist-1-1-2009] |

## 3. Criteria / categories

### Target lesions

| Category | Criterion | Source |
|---|---|---|
| CR | Disappearance of all target lesions; every pathological node (target or not) < 10 mm short axis | [recist-1-1-2009] |
| PR | ≥ 30% decrease in the sum of diameters vs baseline sum | [recist-1-1-2009] |
| PD | ≥ 20% increase in the sum vs the smallest sum on study (nadir, which may be baseline) **and** absolute increase ≥ 5 mm; or new lesion(s) | [recist-1-1-2009] |
| SD | Neither PR nor PD, referenced to the nadir | [recist-1-1-2009] |

### Non-target lesions and new lesions

- Non-target lesions are followed as present, absent or "unequivocal progression". Unequivocal
  progression needs substantial overall worsening; a modest increase is not enough. When only
  non-measurable disease exists, the benchmark is an increase comparable to a 73% volume
  increase (≈ 20% in diameter), e.g. effusion from trace to large. [recist-1-1-2009]
- A new lesion must be unequivocal (not due to technique or non-tumour causes); if equivocal,
  repeat imaging and backdate PD to the first scan if confirmed. [recist-1-1-2009]
- Non-target response categories: CR (all non-target lesions gone, tumour markers normal, nodes
  < 10 mm short axis), non-CR/non-PD, and PD. A new lesion in an area not imaged at baseline
  also counts as PD. [recist-1-1-2009]
- FDG-PET ("positive" = uptake > 2× the surrounding tissue): negative baseline PET and positive
  follow-up PET = PD; no baseline PET and a positive follow-up PET = PD if CT confirms a new
  site; if CT does not confirm it, follow-up CT, and the PD date is that of the first PET once
  confirmed; a positive PET at a pre-existing site that is not progressing on CT is **not** PD.
  [recist-1-1-2009, §4.3]

### Overall response at each time point (Tables 1–2, summarized)

- Target disease present: CR + non-target CR + no new lesion = CR; target CR with non-target
  non-CR/non-PD or not evaluated = PR; PR (non-target not PD or not all evaluated) = PR; SD
  likewise = SD; target not all evaluated (non-target not PD) = NE; PD in target or non-target,
  or any new lesion = PD. [recist-1-1-2009, Table 1]
- Non-target disease only: CR = CR; non-CR/non-PD (preferred over "SD" here) = non-CR/non-PD;
  not all evaluated = NE; unequivocal PD or any new lesion = PD. [recist-1-1-2009, Table 2]
- Nodes that return below 10 mm keep a recorded measurement, so CR can coexist with a non-zero
  sum. "Symptomatic deterioration" is a reason to stop therapy, not a response category.
  [recist-1-1-2009, §4.4.4]

## 4. Management

- Confirmation (§4.6.1): PR/CR need confirmation (generally ≥ 4 weeks later) only in
  non-randomised trials with response as the primary endpoint; best overall response then
  follows Table 3. SD must meet its criteria at least once after study entry at a protocol
  minimum interval (in general not less than 6–8 weeks). Duration of response runs from first
  CR/PR to documented progression; SD duration from treatment start (randomisation) to
  progression, with the smallest sum on study as reference (§4.6.2–4.6.3). Re-evaluation every
  6–8 weeks is reasonable in phase II (§4.5). [recist-1-1-2009]
- Not a treatment guideline. Confirmation of PR/CR is required only in non-randomised trials with
  response as the primary endpoint. [recist-1-1-2009]

## 5. Pitfalls

- Nodes are measured by **short axis** and keep being recorded even below 10 mm; CR can coexist
  with a non-zero sum because of nodes. [recist-1-1-2009]
- Lesions too small to measure but present get a default 5 mm; a lesion that disappears gets 0.
  [recist-1-1-2009]
- Fragmenting lesions: add the longest diameters of the fragments; coalescing lesions: measure
  the parts while a plane exists, then the whole. [recist-1-1-2009]
- Ultrasound should not be used to measure lesions (not reproducible); new lesions seen at US
  should be confirmed at CT or MRI. [recist-1-1-2009]
- Slice thickness > 5 mm raises the minimum measurable size (twice the slice thickness).

## 6. What the source does not say

- No routine-practice reporting template.

## 7. Disagreements and newer versions

- Changes from RECIST 1.0: target lesions reduced from 10 to 5 (and from 5 to 2 per organ);
  nodes added by short axis; 5-mm absolute increase for PD; PET guidance. [recist-1-1-2009]
