# Incidental gallbladder polyps at US (SRU 2022)

> Morphology-based risk stratification of gallbladder polyps found incidentally at
> ultrasound into **extremely low**, **low** and **indeterminate** risk, each with size
> thresholds for no follow-up, US follow-up or surgical consultation. Rationale: most polyps
> < 10 mm are benign cholesterol polyps, and the overall risk of gallbladder cancer is not
> increased in patients with small polyps. Based on literature, expert opinion and panel
> consensus. [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022]

## 1. Scope

- **Applies to:** gallbladder polyps detected incidentally at US. [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022]
- **Before applying the algorithm** (Figure 1): [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Fig. 1]

| Situation | Action | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Technically inadequate (poor technique, polyp not well seen, gallbladder not distended) | Short-interval US within **1–2 months** with optimized grey-scale, colour Doppler technique and patient preparation | [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Fig. 1] |
| Cannot distinguish > 10 mm tumefactive sludge or adenomyomatosis from a polyp | Short-interval US **or** CEUS or MRI | [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Fig. 1] |
| Suspicion of invasive or malignant tumour is high (wall invasion, concurrent liver masses, malignant biliary obstruction, pathologic porta hepatis or para-aortic nodes) | Referral to oncologic specialist | [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Fig. 1] |
| Primary sclerosing cholangitis | Gastrointestinal specialty guidelines (AGA/ACG) | [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Fig. 1] |
| Otherwise | SRU algorithm | [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Fig. 1] |

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Gallbladder polyp | Solid, non-mobile, non-shadowing protrusion from the mucosa, not attributable to stones, sludge, a mucosal fold or diffuse/focal wall thickening; pedunculated or sessile | [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Table] |
| Pedunculated | Attached to the wall by a stalk or pedicle | [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Table] |
| "Ball-on-the-wall" | Pedunculated, usually round/ovoid polyp barely attached to the wall | [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Table] |
| Sessile | Flat or dome-shaped, no stalk, broad-based attachment | [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Table] |
| Focal wall thickening adjacent to polyp | Localized thickening **≥ 4 mm** next to the polyp, not due to oedema, adenomyomatosis, a fold or underdistension | [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Table] |
| Tumefactive sludge | Biliary precipitate coalesced into a solid "sludge ball" that can mimic a mass or polyp | [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Table] |
| Size | Rounded to the nearest millimetre | [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Fig. 2] |

## 3. Criteria / categories

| Risk | Morphology | Size → management | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Extremely low** | Pedunculated "ball-on-the-wall", or pedunculated with a **thin** stalk | **≤ 9 mm:** no follow-up · **10–14 mm:** US at 6, 12 and 24 months (surgical consult is an acceptable alternative) · **≥ 15 mm:** surgical consult | [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Fig. 2] |
| **Low** | Pedunculated with a **thick or wide** stalk, or **sessile** | **≤ 6 mm:** no follow-up · **7–9 mm:** US at 12 months · **10–14 mm:** US at 6, 12, 24 and 36 months vs surgical consult · **≥ 15 mm:** surgical consult | [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Fig. 2] |
| **Indeterminate** | **Focal wall thickening ≥ 4 mm** adjacent to the polyp | **≤ 6 mm:** US at 6, 12, 24 and 36 months vs surgical consult · **≥ 7 mm:** surgical consult | [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Fig. 2] |

Rules (Figure 2 footnotes): [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, Fig. 2]

- Polyp size is rounded to the nearest millimetre.

- During follow-up: increase of **≥ 4 mm in ≤ 12 months**, or reaching the threshold size of the
  category → surgical consult. Decrease of **≥ 4 mm** → stop following.
- If unsure between categories, choose **low risk**.
- It is optional to treat polyps as low risk instead of extremely low risk when certain
  ethnicities with higher gallbladder cancer incidence are known (North Indian, North/South
  American Indigenous, local incidence).
- The "vs surgical consult" alternatives appear in Fig. 2 (checked on the rendered figure on
  2026-11-12, including footnote c for 10–14 mm extremely low risk); the legends of Figs. 3–7
  mention only the follow-up schedule.

Panel statements in the text: polyp vascularity and echogenicity should **not** change the risk
category; a maximum follow-up of **3 years** is sufficient; growth of up to 3 mm may be natural
history, while ≥ 4 mm in 1 year is "rapid growth"; EUS is not part of the usual work-up; CT is
inferior to CEUS or MRI for separating sludge, polyp and adenomyomatosis.
[sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022, text]

## 4. Management

See section 3. Patient selection for surgery is a shared decision weighing health status,
age-related surgical risk and the imaging risk. [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022]

## 5. Pitfalls

- Calling tumefactive sludge, a fold or adenomyomatosis (comet-tail or twinkling artefact) a
  polyp; use better technique, CEUS or MRI when unsure.
- Following a polyp seen on a poorly distended gallbladder without first repeating the scan.
- Applying the algorithm in PSC or when invasive tumour is suspected.

## 6. What the source does not say

- **Age**, **coexisting gallstones** and **number of polyps** should **not** influence risk
  stratification (lack of evidence). [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022]
- It does not give a 2-mm growth trigger (it notes that a 2-mm increase, as in the European
  guidelines, may be too low a threshold for cholecystectomy). [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022]
- It does not cover polyps found on CT or MRI as such; it is a US-based algorithm.

## 7. Disagreements and newer versions

- **European multisociety guidelines** (ESGAR/EAES/EFISDS/ESGE, Wiles et al, Eur Radiol 2017)
  suggest a 2-mm increase in size as a trigger; the SRU panel cites data that 2-mm growth is
  common and may be too low a threshold for cholecystectomy. [sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022]
- The 2013 ACR white paper (`acr-incidental-gallbladder-2013`) used size alone (≤ 6 mm nothing;
  7–9 mm yearly US; ≥ 10 mm surgical consult).
