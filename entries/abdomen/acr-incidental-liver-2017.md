# Incidental liver lesion on CT (ACR white paper, 2017)

> Algorithm by lesion size, imaging features (benign, suspicious, "flash-filling") and patient
> risk (low vs high) for liver lesions found incidentally on CT. Guidance, not a standard of
> care. [acr-liver-2017]

## 1. Scope

- **Applies to:** adults (≥ 18 years), asymptomatic, CT for an unrelated reason.
  [acr-liver-2017]
- **Does not apply** when the CT was done to evaluate known or suspected liver disease; does
  not cover abscess/infection. [acr-liver-2017]
- Lesion with vascular invasion, biliary dilatation or associated adenopathy → oncologic
  evaluation directly. [acr-liver-2017]

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Low risk | No known malignancy, no liver dysfunction, no hepatic risk factor; within low risk, > 40 years carries more risk than younger patients | [acr-liver-2017] |
| High risk | Known malignancy prone to liver metastasis; cirrhosis; and/or hepatic risk factors (hepatitis, NASH, alcoholism, primary sclerosing cholangitis, primary biliary cirrhosis, choledochal cyst, haemochromatosis and other hereditary disease, anabolic steroid use) | [acr-liver-2017] |
| Benign features | Sharp margin, homogeneous low attenuation (**≤ 20 HU**) unenhanced and/or portal venous, and characteristic patterns of haemangioma, FNH, focal fat/sparing, perfusion change | [acr-liver-2017] |
| Cyst (low risk) | **−10 to +20 HU**, homogeneous, sharp margin, no enhancement, no wall thickening/nodularity, no septa (pseudoenhancement may push > 20 HU) | [acr-liver-2017] |
| Suspicious features | Ill-defined margin, heterogeneous density, mural thickening or nodularity, thick septa, intermediate-to-high attenuation on portal phase (**> 20 HU** without pseudoenhancement); with pre and post phases, enhancement **> 20 HU** | [acr-liver-2017] |
| Flash-filling | Uniform hyperenhancement in arterial (incl. late arterial/early portal) phase with no other phases to characterize | [acr-liver-2017] |

Growth is not formally in the algorithm; **no growth for 1 year** strongly favours benignity.

## 3. Criteria / categories

| Size | Features | Low risk | High risk | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **< 1 cm** | Any (except suspicious) | Benign, no follow-up | **MRI in 3–6 months** | [acr-liver-2017] |
| **1.0–1.5 cm** | Benign | No follow-up | No follow-up | [acr-liver-2017] |
| | Suspicious | **MRI now** | **MRI now** | [acr-liver-2017] |
| | Flash-filling | No follow-up | **MRI now** | [acr-liver-2017] |
| **> 1.5 cm** | Benign | No follow-up | No follow-up | [acr-liver-2017] |
| | Suspicious or flash-filling | **MRI now** | **MRI now or biopsy** | [acr-liver-2017] |

## 4. Management

- < 1 cm in low risk **with** suspicious features: consider MRI (the radiologist may depart
  from the rule). [acr-liver-2017]
- ≥ 1 cm without enough imaging to decide (e.g. unenhanced CT only) → MRI now.
- High-risk 3–6-month MRI may need to be earlier; in oncologic patients already on routine CT,
  CT is acceptable.
- > 1.5 cm without benign features: at least MRI now; direct biopsy may be appropriate (e.g.
  3 cm probable colorectal metastasis). Core biopsy preferred over FNA.
- FNH vs adenoma matters when **> 3 cm and subcapsular** → MRI with **gadoxetate**; otherwise
  extracellular contrast is preferred. [acr-liver-2017]

## 5. Pitfalls

- Cystic metastases (ovary, GIST) can mimic cysts. [acr-liver-2017]
- Pseudoenhancement can raise a benign cyst above 20 HU.

## 6. What the source does not say

- It is not the system for patients at risk of HCC in practice; LI-RADS is the specific,
  newer system for that population (cirrhosis, chronic HBV, prior HCC).
