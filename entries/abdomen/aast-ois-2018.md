# AAST Organ Injury Scale 2018 — spleen, liver, kidney

> The 2018 revision adds **CT-detected vascular injury** (pseudoaneurysm or arteriovenous fistula)
> and **active bleeding** to the grades. Any vascular injury or contained active bleeding makes
> the spleen grade IV, the liver grade III and the kidney grade III; active bleeding beyond the
> organ makes the spleen grade V, the liver grade IV and the kidney grade IV. Dual-phase CT
> (arterial and portal venous) is recommended, plus an excretory phase when renal injury is
> suspected. [aast-ois-2018]

## 1. Scope

- **Applies to:** traumatic injury of spleen, liver and kidney graded on CT (or at operation or
  pathology). Final grade = highest of imaging, operative and pathologic criteria.
- **Does not address:** treatment (the authors state this explicitly).

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Vascular injury | Pseudoaneurysm or arteriovenous fistula: focal collection of vascular contrast that **decreases** in attenuation on delayed imaging | [aast-ois-2018] |
| Active bleeding | Vascular contrast, focal or diffuse, that **increases** in size or attenuation on the delayed phase | [aast-ois-2018] |
| Multiple injuries | Multiple grade I–II injuries → advance one grade, up to grade III (kidney: bilateral injuries) | [aast-ois-2018] |

## 3. Criteria / categories (CT criteria, summarized)

### Spleen

| Grade | CT findings | Source |
|---|---|---|
| I | Subcapsular hematoma < 10% of surface; laceration < 1 cm deep; capsular tear | [aast-ois-2018, Table 1] |
| II | Subcapsular hematoma 10–50%; intraparenchymal hematoma < 5 cm; laceration 1–3 cm | [aast-ois-2018, Table 1] |
| III | Subcapsular hematoma > 50%; ruptured subcapsular or intraparenchymal hematoma ≥ 5 cm; laceration > 3 cm | [aast-ois-2018, Table 1] |
| IV | Any injury with splenic vascular injury or active bleeding confined to the capsule; laceration of segmental/hilar vessels with > 25% devascularization | [aast-ois-2018, Table 1] |
| V | Any injury with vascular injury and active bleeding beyond the spleen into the peritoneum; shattered spleen | [aast-ois-2018, Table 1] |

### Liver

| Grade | CT findings | Source |
|---|---|---|
| I | Subcapsular hematoma < 10%; laceration < 1 cm deep; capsular tear | [aast-ois-2018, Table 2] |
| II | Subcapsular hematoma 10–50%; intraparenchymal hematoma < 10 cm; laceration 1–3 cm deep and ≤ 10 cm long | [aast-ois-2018, Table 2] |
| III | Subcapsular hematoma > 50% or ruptured; intraparenchymal hematoma > 10 cm; laceration > 3 cm deep; any injury with liver vascular injury or active bleeding contained in the parenchyma | [aast-ois-2018, Table 2] |
| IV | Parenchymal disruption of 25–75% of a hepatic lobe; active bleeding beyond the liver into the peritoneum | [aast-ois-2018, Table 2] |
| V | Disruption > 75% of a lobe; juxtahepatic venous injury (retrohepatic IVC, central major hepatic veins) | [aast-ois-2018, Table 2] |

### Kidney

| Grade | CT findings | Source |
|---|---|---|
| I | Subcapsular hematoma and/or contusion without laceration | [aast-ois-2018, Table 3] |
| II | Perirenal hematoma confined to Gerota fascia; laceration ≤ 1 cm without urinary extravasation | [aast-ois-2018, Table 3] |
| III | Laceration > 1 cm without collecting-system rupture or extravasation; any injury with vascular injury or active bleeding contained within Gerota fascia | [aast-ois-2018, Table 3] |
| IV | Laceration into the collecting system with urinary extravasation; renal pelvis laceration and/or complete ureteropelvic disruption; segmental renal artery or vein injury; active bleeding beyond Gerota fascia; segmental or complete infarction from thrombosis without active bleeding | [aast-ois-2018, Table 3] |
| V | Main renal artery or vein laceration or hilar avulsion; devascularized kidney with active bleeding; shattered kidney with loss of identifiable anatomy | [aast-ois-2018, Table 3] |

## 4. Management

- Not addressed by the scale. The authors note vascular injury is associated with higher
  failure of nonoperative management and may prompt angioembolization.

## 5. Pitfalls

- Grading from a single portal venous phase: pseudoaneurysm vs active bleeding requires the
  delayed behaviour of the contrast.
- Forgetting to upgrade for multiple low-grade injuries (only up to grade III).
- Calling a kidney grade I–III when the excretory phase (not acquired) would show extravasation.

## 6. What the source does not say

- It does not define how to estimate "% of surface area" or "% of lobe" quantitatively.
- Other organs (pancreas, bowel, adrenal) are not in this revision.

## 7. Disagreements and newer versions

- Replaces the 1989 (spleen, liver, kidney) and 1994 (spleen, liver) OIS.
