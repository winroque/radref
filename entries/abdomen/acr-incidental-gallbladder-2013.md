# Incidental gallbladder and biliary findings (ACR white paper, 2013)

> Recommendations for gallbladder and bile duct findings seen incidentally on abdominal/pelvic
> CT or MRI. Not a complete review; symptomatic patients are outside it. Guidance, not a
> standard of care. [acr-gallbladder-2013]

## 1. Scope

- **Applies to:** incidental gallbladder and biliary findings on CT or MRI in asymptomatic
  patients. [acr-gallbladder-2013]

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Biliary dilatation | CBD or common hepatic duct **> 6 mm** in patients **< 60 years** with a gallbladder, or **> 10 mm** after cholecystectomy; **> 7 mm** suggests obstruction in patients with a gallbladder. Measure the **short axis** (oblique cuts overestimate). The summary table simplifies to "> 6 mm without cholecystectomy" | [acr-gallbladder-2013] |
| Distended gallbladder | **> 4 cm transverse and > 9 cm longitudinal** | [acr-gallbladder-2013] |
| Wall thickening | **> 3 mm** (diffuse or focal) | [acr-gallbladder-2013] |
| Dense content | **20–100 HU** (sludge, excreted contrast, concentrated bile, haemorrhage, non-calcified stone) | [acr-gallbladder-2013] |

## 3. Criteria / categories

| Finding | Recommendation | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Stones, no mass, no duct dilatation, no symptoms | Nothing; US if symptoms develop | [acr-gallbladder-2013] |
| Wall calcification (focal or diffuse "porcelain"), no mass | No follow-up; if the referrer wants follow-up, individualize and use contrast CT (US is poor with concentric calcification). Cancer risk low (~5–7% in series; higher in focal/mucosal than diffuse) | [acr-gallbladder-2013] |
| Dense content (20–100 HU) | No work-up/follow-up if no wall thickening or pericholecystic change | [acr-gallbladder-2013] |
| Diffuse wall thickening > 3 mm, no mass | No specific work-up (hepatitis, heart failure, liver disease, pancreatitis, hypoproteinaemia) | [acr-gallbladder-2013] |
| Focal thickening > 3 mm or mass | Depends on size and clinical context; adenomyomatosis has a typical US appearance (comet-tail); no follow-up if asymptomatic and benign | [acr-gallbladder-2013] |
| Polyp **≤ 6 mm** | Nothing | [acr-gallbladder-2013] |
| Polyp **7–9 mm** | **Yearly US**; growth → surgical evaluation | [acr-gallbladder-2013] |
| Polyp **≥ 10 mm** or mass | Surgical evaluation / consider cholecystectomy; without surgery → yearly US | [acr-gallbladder-2013] |
| Pericholecystic fluid (outside the wall) | Individualize (usually symptomatic; do not confuse with wall oedema) | [acr-gallbladder-2013] |
| Distended gallbladder | Asymptomatic: fasting, nothing. Symptomatic: consider obstruction | [acr-gallbladder-2013] |
| Biliary dilatation | Normal labs → nothing; abnormal → ERCP, EUS, MRCP or CT cholangiography | [acr-gallbladder-2013] |

## 4. Management

- With normal alkaline phosphatase and bilirubin, mild dilatation needs no imaging.
- Suspected distal CBD mass: EUS or ERCP-guided FNA first. [acr-gallbladder-2013]

## 5. Pitfalls

- Oblique measurement of the CBD overestimates calibre.
- Mistaking wall oedema for pericholecystic fluid.

## 6. What the source does not say

- It gives no polyp morphology risk categories (introduced later by the SRU 2022 consensus).

## 7. Disagreements and newer versions

- **Gallbladder polyps:** the SRU 2022 consensus (`sru-gallbladder-polyps-2022`) replaced the
  single 10 mm cut-off with morphology-based risk categories; European guidelines
  (ESGAR/EAES/EFISDS/ESGE 2022) are cited by the SRU paper as keeping cholecystectomy at ≥ 10 mm.
  For polyps, the 2013 thresholds above are superseded in practice by the newer documents.
