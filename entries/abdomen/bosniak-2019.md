# Bosniak classification of cystic renal masses, version 2019

> Risk stratification of **cystic** renal masses at **CT or MRI** in five classes (I, II, IIF,
> III, IV). Version 2019 formally adds MRI, gives quantitative definitions for previously vague
> terms (thin, minimally thickened, thick, few, many, irregular, nodule), uses a single
> definition of enhancement for all classes, brings incompletely characterized but highly
> likely benign masses into class II, and moves more masses into lower classes. Endorsed by
> the Society of Abdominal Radiology; presented as an **update proposal** that requires
> validation before widespread implementation. [bosniak-2019]

## 1. Scope

- **Applies to:** cystic renal masses at CT or MRI, after **infectious, inflammatory or
  vascular** aetiologies and **necrotic solid masses** are excluded. [bosniak-2019, Table 2]
- **Cystic mass:** less than approximately **25%** of the mass is enhancing tissue. More than
  that is a solid mass, outside Bosniak. [bosniak-2019]
- **Hereditary RCC syndromes** (von Hippel-Lindau, hereditary leiomyomatosis and RCC, others):
  benign-appearing cysts may be or become cancer; the reporting phrases are intended for the
  general population, not these patients. [bosniak-2019, Table 4]
- **Ultrasound:** other than diagnosing a Bosniak I simple cyst (anechoic, well-defined smooth
  borders, increased through-transmission), the role of US with or without contrast in
  assigning a class is **uncertain**. Masses that are not simple cysts at US generally warrant
  renal mass protocol CT or MRI, possibly except those with few thin septa (II).
  [bosniak-2019, Table 2 footnote; Knowledge Gaps]

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Definition (v2019) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Enhancement (all classes) | Unequivocally **perceived** (clearly visible when unenhanced and enhanced images with the same technique are compared) **or** **measurable**: increase **≥ 20 HU** at CT, or **≥ 15%** signal intensity at MRI. A feature not clearly enhancing and too small for a conventionally sized ROI is considered **non-enhancing** | [bosniak-2019, Table 3] |
| Homogeneous | Similar attenuation, signal or echogenicity throughout; thin wall allowed; **no septa or calcifications**; check for subtle or peripheral heterogeneous elements | [bosniak-2019, Table 3] |
| Simple fluid | CT: homogeneous **−9 to 20 HU** (unenhanced or enhanced). MRI: homogeneous, similar to CSF on T2. US: anechoic with increased through-transmission | [bosniak-2019, Table 3] |
| Septum | Linear or curvilinear structure connecting two surfaces | [bosniak-2019, Table 3] |
| Few / many septa | Few = **1–3**; many = **≥ 4** ("more than a few" removed) | [bosniak-2019, Table 3] |
| Thin | **≤ 2 mm** (replaces "hairline"/"pencil thin") | [bosniak-2019, Table 3] |
| Minimally thickened | **3 mm** | [bosniak-2019, Table 3] |
| Thick | **≥ 4 mm** | [bosniak-2019, Table 3] |
| Irregular thickening | Focal or diffuse enhancing convex protrusion(s) **≤ 3 mm** with **obtuse** margins | [bosniak-2019, Table 3] |
| Nodule | Focal enhancing convex protrusion of **any size with acute margins**, or **≥ 4 mm with obtuse margins** | [bosniak-2019, Table 3] |
| Measuring protrusions | Perpendicular to the wall/septum of origin; protrusions on both sides are added, excluding the wall/septum thickness | [bosniak-2019, Fig. 3] |
| Too small to characterize | Section thickness more than half the mass diameter (Nyquist); also intrarenal masses < 1.5 cm next to avidly enhancing parenchyma (pseudoenhancement) | [bosniak-2019] |

Quantitative thresholds are "guideposts rather than absolute expressions"; 1-mm differences may
not be measurable reliably. [bosniak-2019]

## 3. Criteria / categories

| Class | CT | MRI | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | Well-defined, thin (≤ 2 mm) smooth wall; homogeneous simple fluid (−9 to 20 HU); no septa or calcifications; wall may enhance | Same, fluid similar to CSF | [bosniak-2019, Table 2] |
| **II** | Six types, all well-defined with thin smooth walls: (1) few (1–3) thin septa, septa and wall may enhance, calcification of any type; (2) homogeneous **≥ 70 HU** at unenhanced CT; (3) homogeneous non-enhancing **> 20 HU** at renal mass protocol CT, calcification of any type; (4) homogeneous **−9 to 20 HU** at unenhanced CT; (5) homogeneous **21–30 HU** at portal venous phase; (6) homogeneous low-attenuation mass too small to characterize | Three types, all well-defined with thin smooth walls: (1) few (1–3) thin **enhancing** septa, any non-enhancing septa, calcification of any type; (2) homogeneous, markedly T2-hyperintense (similar to CSF) at unenhanced MRI; (3) homogeneous, markedly T1-hyperintense (≈ **2.5×** normal parenchyma) at unenhanced MRI | [bosniak-2019, Table 2] |
| **IIF** | Smooth minimally thickened (3 mm) **enhancing** wall, or smooth minimal thickening (3 mm) of one or more enhancing septa, or **many (≥ 4)** smooth thin enhancing septa | Two types: (1) same as CT; (2) cystic mass **heterogeneously** hyperintense on unenhanced fat-saturated T1 (not meeting III/IV) | [bosniak-2019, Table 2] |
| **III** | One or more enhancing **thick (≥ 4 mm)** or enhancing **irregular** (≤ 3 mm obtuse protrusion) walls or septa | Same | [bosniak-2019, Table 2] |
| **IV** | One or more enhancing **nodules** (≥ 4 mm obtuse, or any size with acute margins) | Same | [bosniak-2019, Table 2] |

Rules: [bosniak-2019, Table 2 footnotes; text, pp. 482–483 for calcification and the 3-cm limit]

- Features of more than one class → assign the **highest** class (e.g. few thick enhancing
  septa = III, not II).
- A rare combination of features not fitting any class may warrant **IIF**.
- **Calcification of any morphology** is now a class II feature (no longer IIF on its own).
- The **> 3 cm** size limit for homogeneous hyperattenuating non-enhancing masses was removed.
- Consider MRI before classifying CT masses with abundant thick or nodular calcification,
  homogeneous hyperattenuating non-enhancing masses > 3 cm, or heterogeneous masses (e.g. many
  non-enhancing septa, or non-enhancing septa/wall ≥ 3 mm) — occult enhancement may change the
  class. Heterogeneous non-enhancing masses at CT are incompletely characterized.

## 4. Management

The update "remains a malignancy prediction system, not a comprehensive management
algorithm"; patient factors decide treatment. [bosniak-2019]

| Class | Malignancy (from the text) | General management / suggested report phrase | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | — | Benign simple cyst, no follow-up | [bosniak-2019, Table 4] |
| II | < 1%, likely indistinguishable from 0 | No follow-up ("benign Bosniak II renal cyst" when confirmed cyst; "likely benign Bosniak II renal mass" when incompletely characterized) | [bosniak-2019, Table 4] |
| IIF | Unknown (reported 0–38%); ~10% progress, and ~85% of those are malignant | Imaging at **6 and 12 months, then annually for a total of 5 years**, looking for morphologic change | [bosniak-2019, Table 4] |
| III | ~50% (series 25–100%) | Intermediate probability; consider urology consultation. Observation vs treatment in the general population is unclear | [bosniak-2019, Table 4] |
| IV | ~90% (series 56–100%) | Large majority malignant; consider urology consultation; resection or ablation considered | [bosniak-2019, Table 4] |

- Use "cyst" only for class I and for class II masses diagnosed as cysts; otherwise "cystic
  mass". Avoid "complicated cyst" and "complex cyst". Cite the version used ("Bosniak
  Classification, version 2019"). [bosniak-2019]

## 5. Pitfalls

- Applying Bosniak to a solid mass (≥ ~25% enhancing tissue) or to a necrotic solid tumour
  (necrosis tends to be central with a thick peripheral rind).
- Assigning IIF/III/IV from B-mode US.
- Measuring enhancement in structures too small for an ROI (pixel-wise assessment is error
  prone): those are non-enhancing unless clearly visibly enhancing.
- Calling a 30 HU homogeneous lesion at portal venous phase indeterminate: 21–30 HU is class II
  (the paper notes ≤ 40 HU is likely benign but 30 HU appears safe).
- Calling a mass with few septa class II without checking septal thickness and enhancement.
- Rare cancers (e.g. non-enhancing RCC) may be misclassified; radiologists may deviate from the
  system with clinical judgement. [bosniak-2019]

## 6. What the source does not say

- No size or growth-rate criterion: "Size and growth rate are not included"; during
  surveillance, change in morphology or growth of solid elements matters more than overall
  size. [bosniak-2019]
- It does not distinguish indolent from aggressive cancers.
- It does not classify masses at B-mode US beyond class I; contrast-enhanced US upgraded about
  one quarter of masses in prior studies but is not incorporated. [bosniak-2019]
- It does not validate the quantitative thresholds; it is a proposal.

## 7. Disagreements and newer versions

- The ACR incidental renal mass white paper (`acr-renal-2018`) uses the **pre-2019**
  classification (Table 1 of this paper): e.g. IIF included masses with thick/nodular
  calcification and hyperattenuating masses > 3 cm; v2019 moves both to class II when
  otherwise qualifying.
- American Urological Association guidelines cited in the paper still supported surgery for
  III and IV masses ≥ 2 cm in patients without limited life expectancy. [bosniak-2019]
