# UTD classification (2014)

> Six US features define urinary tract (UT) dilation: anteroposterior renal pelvic diameter
> (APRPD), calyceal dilation (central vs peripheral), parenchymal thickness, parenchymal
> appearance, ureter and bladder (plus unexplained oligohydramnios before birth). The **most
> concerning feature** sets the category. Normal APRPD: **< 4 mm before 28 weeks, < 7 mm from
> 28 weeks, < 10 mm postnatally**. [nguyen-utd-2014]

## 1. Scope

- **Applies to:** isolated fetal (A = antenatal) and postnatal (P) urinary tract dilation.
- **Does not apply to:** solitary, ectopic or multicystic dysplastic kidney, or cystic renal
  disease; the follow-up scheme does not apply after surgery. [nguyen-utd-2014, p. 988]
- Terminology: use "UT dilation"; avoid "hydronephrosis", "pyelectasis" and similar terms
  (Recommendation 1). [nguyen-utd-2014]
- Consensus of ACR, AIUM, ASPN, SFU, SMFM, SPU, SPR and SRU. [nguyen-utd-2014]

## 2. Definitions

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| APRPD | Transverse plane, maximal diameter of the **intrarenal** pelvis; fetal spine at 12 or 6 o'clock; postnatally at the hilum, prone encouraged, same position in serial exams | [nguyen-utd-2014] |
| Calyceal dilation | Central vs peripheral; hard to separate before the third trimester | [nguyen-utd-2014] |
| Parenchymal appearance | Echogenicity vs liver/spleen, cortical cysts, corticomedullary differentiation (postnatal) | [nguyen-utd-2014] |
| Ureter | A visible dilated ureter is abnormal; transient visualization is normal postnatally | [nguyen-utd-2014] |
| Bladder | Wall thickening, ureterocele, dilated posterior urethra | [nguyen-utd-2014] |

## 3. Criteria / categories

### Prenatal

| Category | Findings | Source |
|---|---|---|
| UTD A1 (low risk) | APRPD 4 to < 7 mm (16–27 weeks) or 7 to < 10 mm (≥ 28 weeks); central or no calyceal dilation; normal parenchyma, ureter, bladder; no unexplained oligohydramnios | [nguyen-utd-2014] |
| UTD A2–3 (increased risk) | APRPD ≥ 7 mm (< 28 weeks) or ≥ 10 mm (≥ 28 weeks), **or** any of: peripheral calyceal dilation, abnormal parenchymal thickness or appearance, dilated ureter, abnormal bladder, unexplained oligohydramnios | [nguyen-utd-2014] |

### Postnatal (first US > 48 h after birth)

| Category | Findings | Source |
|---|---|---|
| UTD P1 (low risk) | APRPD 10 to < 15 mm, or central calyceal dilation (even with APRPD < 10 mm); normal parenchyma, ureter, bladder | [nguyen-utd-2014] |
| UTD P2 (intermediate) | APRPD ≥ 15 mm, or peripheral calyceal dilation, or dilated ureter; normal parenchyma and bladder | [nguyen-utd-2014] |
| UTD P3 (high risk) | As P2 plus abnormal parenchyma (thinning, increased echogenicity, reduced corticomedullary differentiation) or abnormal bladder — regardless of APRPD | [nguyen-utd-2014] |

- Bilateral cases: classify by the more severely affected side. [nguyen-utd-2014]
- Approximate equivalence: SFU 1–2 = P1, SFU 3 = P2, SFU 4 = P3. [nguyen-utd-2014]

## 4. Management (proposed scheme)

| Category | Follow-up | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A1 before 32 weeks | Repeat US at ≥ 32 weeks; resolved with normal findings → no further follow-up; persistent → two postnatal US (> 48 h to 1 month, then 1–6 months later) | [nguyen-utd-2014] |
| A2–3 | Repeat prenatal US in 4–6 weeks; postnatal US > 48 h and before 1 month (sooner if suspected posterior urethral valves or bilateral disease); prenatal specialist consultation when risk is substantial | [nguyen-utd-2014] |
| P1 | US in 1–6 months; VCUG and antibiotic prophylaxis at clinician discretion | [nguyen-utd-2014] |
| P2 | US in 1–3 months; VCUG, antibiotics and functional scan at clinician discretion | [nguyen-utd-2014] |
| P3 | US within 1 month; VCUG and antibiotic prophylaxis recommended (depending on suspected pathology); functional scan at discretion | [nguyen-utd-2014] |

- Report: the **seven** imaging parameters (including unexplained oligohydramnios; Table 3) in the body and the UTD category plus suggested management in the
  impression. [nguyen-utd-2014]

## 5. Pitfalls

- Postnatal US within the first 48 h underestimates dilation, partly from dehydration; earlier
  scanning is reserved for oligohydramnios, urethral obstruction, bilateral high-grade dilation or
  doubtful follow-up. [nguyen-utd-2014]
- Measuring the extrarenal pelvis. [nguyen-utd-2014]
- Changing patient position between serial exams (APRPD often decreases prone); use the same
  position each time. Rescan after bladder emptying when dilation is present. [nguyen-utd-2014]
- Modifiers: worsening on serial exams raises risk; sex and laterality have insufficient
  evidence except posterior urethral valves in boys (Recommendation 5). Isolated fetal
  pyelectasis is associated with trisomy 21 (LR 1.5–1.6). In A2–3, later prenatal intervals are
  left to the clinician. [nguyen-utd-2014]

## 6. What the source does not say

- No category for isolated ureteral dilation without pelvic dilation beyond treating the ureter as
  a modifier (visible ureter → A2–3 or P2).

## 7. Disagreements and newer versions

- Initially three prenatal groups were planned; A2 and A3 were merged because central vs
  peripheral calyces are hard to separate prenatally. [nguyen-utd-2014]
